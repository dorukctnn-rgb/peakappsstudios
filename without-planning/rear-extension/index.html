How far can I extend without planning permission? 3 m, 4 m, 6 m or 8 m

In England a single-storey rear extension can go 3 m beyond the original rear wall of a semi-detached or terraced house, or 4 m for a detached house, without planning permission, and 6 m or 8 m with the larger home extension prior approval; it must be no higher than 4 m, with eaves no higher than 3 m within 2 m of a boundary.

Rules as of 10 October 2026. GPDO 2015, Schedule 2, Part 1, Class A.

Check your extension in 3D

  • Single storey: 3 m beyond the rear wall (semi-detached or terraced), 4 m (detached), and no higher than 4 m (A.1(f)).
  • With prior approval: 6 m and 8 m, except on article 2(3) land or an SSSI (A.1(g), A.4).
  • More than one storey: 3 m deep at most and 7 m from the boundary opposite the rear wall (A.1(h)).
  • Within 2 m of a boundary: eaves no higher than 3 m (A.1(i)).
  • Measured from the rear wall of the original house: as built, or as it stood on 1 July 1948.

The larger home extension: prior approval

Between 3 m and 6 m (or 4 m and 8 m for a detached house), a single-storey rear extension is permitted only after you tell the council. Send a written description with how far the extension goes beyond the rear wall, its maximum height and eaves height, a plan of the site, the addresses of the adjoining premises and your contact address. The council serves notice on the neighbours, who get at least 21 days to comment; you may start once the council says prior approval is not required or is given, or after 42 days without a decision (paragraph A.4). The fee is £249 from 1 April 2026.

Earlier extensions count

Limits apply to the total enlargement when a new extension joins an earlier one (paragraph A.1(ja)). The technical guidance gives the example of a detached house with a 4 m single-storey extension: adding another 5 m makes 9 m, which is more than 8 m, so it needs planning permission. A first floor over an existing single-storey extension deeper than 3 m fails for the same reason.

Side extensions are limited to one storey, 4 m in height and half the width of the original house, and are not permitted on article 2(3) land (conservation areas, National Parks, National Landscapes, the Broads, World Heritage Sites).

Questions

How far can I extend a semi-detached house without planning permission?

3 m beyond the original rear wall for a single-storey extension, or up to 6 m with the larger home extension prior approval, no higher than 4 m. A two-storey extension can go 3 m and must be at least 7 m from the rear boundary.

How far can I extend a detached house without planning permission?

4 m for a single-storey rear extension, or up to 8 m with prior approval, no higher than 4 m.

Does the 3 m include my existing extension?

Yes, when the new extension joins it: the limits apply to the total enlargement. Depth is measured from the rear wall of the original house, as built or as it stood on 1 July 1948.
