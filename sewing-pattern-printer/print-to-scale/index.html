Guide · Scale

Print a PDF pattern to scale and check the test square

Short answer: choose 100% or Actual size in the print dialog, never Fit or Shrink, and pick the paper that's really in the printer. Print only the page with the test square, measure it with a ruler, and print the rest only when it's right.

The setting to choose in each app

Every app words it differently. These are the labels as they appear in each print dialog.

Adobe describes Fit as reducing or enlarging each page "to fit the printable area of the currently selected paper size", and Shrink oversized pages as shrinking large pages to the paper. Both quietly change the scale of a pattern. Actual size "prints without scaling", and anything that doesn't fit on the paper is cropped, which is why the paper size matters too.

Read the test square

Measure the square with a rigid ruler rather than a tape measure, along the middle of the square rather than the corners. Work out the scale as measured size ÷ stated size × 100. A millimetre either way can come from the ruler and the paper; a consistent difference of 2% or more is a setting.

US Letter is 215.9 × 279.4 mm, A4 is 210 × 297 mm. Fitting one onto the other scales by the tighter side: 279.4 ÷ 297 = 94.1% for A4 on Letter, 210 ÷ 215.9 = 97.3% for Letter on A4.

A4 pattern, Letter paper (and the other way round)

A4 is 6 mm narrower and 17.6 mm taller than US Letter. Many designers keep their pattern inside the area the two sizes share, so an A4 file printed at 100% on Letter loses only margin. If the pattern runs to the edge of an A4 page, printing it on Letter at 100% cuts off up to 17.6 mm, and fitting it shrinks the pattern by 6%. Use the file made for your paper, or tile the copy-shop file onto your own paper size.

When 100% still prints small on Windows

Some printers apply their own fit-to-page setting after the app has sent the page. Open the printer's Properties or Preferences from the print dialog and look for a scaling, fit to page or borderless option. In one Adobe community thread the fix was replacing the generic Windows printer driver with the printer maker's own driver, after which Acrobat's 100% printed true.

Before you print: one size or all of them?

A layered pattern prints every size unless the app you use can switch layers off. Acrobat Reader on a computer can; Preview, iPad apps and Chrome's viewer can't. Print only your size saves a copy with just the sizes you choose, at exactly the original page size, so the test square stays true. Its Pro tiling adds a first page with a 1 in and a 5 cm square to check before you print everything.
