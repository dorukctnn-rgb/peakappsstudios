Dropped kerb rules: depth, width, visibility and junctions

To get a dropped kerb, most councils want a parking space 4.75 m to 5 m deep and 2.4 m to 3.5 m wide inside your boundary, the crossing 10 m from a junction, up to 15 m on main roads, nothing above 0.6 m beside the opening, and room round any street tree. The law leaves the detail to each council, so the figures below come from each one’s own pages.

Rules read from 13 councils’ own pages on 11 October 2026.

Check your driveway against your council’s rules

  • Depth: 4.75 m to 5 m for a car at right angles; 6 m in front of a garage at Kent, Surrey, Hampshire and Bristol.
  • Width: 2.4 m to 3.5 m; Birmingham asks for 2.75 m for one car and 4.5 m for two.
  • Junctions: 10 m from a junction, up to 15 m on main roads.
  • Visibility: nothing above 0.6 m in a triangle about 2 m each way beside the opening.
  • Trees: clearances from 1 m to 2.5 m from the trunk, 4 times the trunk circumference, or 12 times its diameter.

The law behind every council’s rules

Section 184 of the Highways Act 1980 lets anyone ask the highway authority to build a vehicle crossing, and lets the authority approve it, change it, offer something else or refuse. In deciding, it has to think about damage to the footway or verge, safe access and egress, and the flow of traffic. The numbers are each council’s own way of applying that.

The depth runs from the back of the footway to the nearest part of the house where the car parks. Surrey counts steps, bay windows and porches, Waltham Forest steps, bays and planters, and Hertfordshire utility cabinets and car chargers as well. Lancashire allows no part of the car to overhang, and Hertfordshire, Havering and Birmingham all say they size the space for a bigger car later, not only the one you own now.

A tree rule written as 4 times the circumference gives a 3.6 m radius for a trunk 0.9 m round; written as 12 times the diameter, it gives 3.4 m for the same tree. Surrey, Hertfordshire, Lancashire and Waltham Forest say healthy street trees are not removed for a dropped kerb, and Manchester that it will not damage a tree for one; Havering removes a tree only on an arboricultural report, Bristol only in exceptional cases, and where Birmingham approves a removal you pay for it and the replanting.

Questions

What is the minimum depth for a dropped kerb?

Among the councils checked here, 4.75 m to 5 m from the back of the pavement to the house for a car at right angles, and 6 m where the car parks in front of a garage at Kent, Surrey, Hampshire and Bristol.

How close to a junction can a dropped kerb be?

Usually no closer than 10 m, and up to 15 m on main roads or near busy junctions. Hampshire also refuses any crossing within the curved radius kerb of a junction, while Lancashire says the distance is unlikely to matter on 30 mph residential streets unless there is a specific hazard.

Can I get a dropped kerb with a tree outside?

Usually only if the crossing stays clear of the tree’s roots. The councils checked here want at least 1 m to 2.5 m from the trunk, 4 times its circumference, or 12 times its diameter, and several, Surrey, Hertfordshire and Lancashire among them, will not remove a healthy street tree for one.
