Can I get a dropped kerb? Check your driveway in 3D

You can get a dropped kerb if your council agrees the front garden is deep and wide enough for a car, the access is safe, and trees and street lights are far enough away. Check your measurements against your council’s own published rules before you pay its fee, which is usually non-refundable.

Planning permission is a separate question

The highway authority’s consent and planning permission are separate. A house on an unclassified road usually needs none, if the hard surface in front is permeable or drains to the garden.

Questions

Does a pass here mean the council will approve my dropped kerb?

No. It means nothing you entered breaks a rule the council publishes. The council decides after its own site visit, and judges trees, utility covers, parking bays, traffic orders and visibility on site.

Do I need planning permission for a dropped kerb?

Not usually for a house on an unclassified road. Planning permission is needed for access on to an A, B or C road, for a flat or maisonette, where an Article 4 direction or a planning condition removes the rights, and for more than 5 m² of impermeable surface in front of the house that drains to the road. Some councils also treat conservation areas and listed buildings as needing it.

Which councils does the tool cover?

The rules of Kent, Hampshire, Surrey, Hertfordshire, Lancashire, Birmingham, Leeds, Manchester, Sheffield, Bristol, Havering and Waltham Forest, read from each council’s own pages on 11 October 2026, and Essex’s criteria. Every other council in England gets the national rules and a link to its own page.

Can I park across my own dropped kerb?

In England, parking beside a dropped footway is prohibited in a special enforcement area, where the council enforces it, but one exception is parking outside a home by the occupier or with the occupier’s consent given free, not at a shared driveway (Traffic Management Act 2004, s.86(3)). Councils differ in how they enforce it.

Is anything I enter sent to a server?

No. The check and the 3D model run in your browser. The share link keeps the frontage after the # sign, which browsers do not send to servers. If you buy Pro, only the licence key is sent to Gumroad to check it.

What about Wales, Scotland and Northern Ireland?

They are coming next. Wales shares the Highways Act 1980 but has its own planning rules, Scotland uses the Roads (Scotland) Act 1984, and Northern Ireland has its own roads law; the tool does not guess them.
