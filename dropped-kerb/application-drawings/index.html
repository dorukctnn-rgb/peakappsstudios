Dropped kerb application drawings: what councils ask for, with an example

For the highway application, councils want a sketch or plan of the frontage with its measurements, and several also ask for photographs, the planning position and the owner’s written consent if you do not own the property. A planning application, if you need one, has its own drawing requirements.

What each council asks for, read on 11 October 2026.

Make the plan for your frontage

  • Kent: at least one photograph of the front of the house, taking in the neighbours, and a sketch with the dimensions and all street furniture.
  • Sheffield: a sketch with measurements and your ownership outlined in red.
  • Waltham Forest: the depth of the front garden, the crossover widths at the boundary and at the footway edge, the footway width and the frontage either side.
  • Hertfordshire: a good quality plan; poor plans are returned and the application put on hold.

How to measure

  1. Depth: from the back of the footway to the nearest part of the house where the car parks, counting bay windows, porches, steps and anything else fixed.
  2. Width: the clear space between walls, fences or the house where the car goes.
  3. Footway and verge: from the kerb face to the back of the footway, and any grass in between.
  4. Distances: from where the sloping kerbs would end to each tree trunk, lamp column, sign or cabinet, and to the nearest junction.
  5. Heights: any wall, fence or hedge beside the opening, against the 0.6 m visibility limit.

Questions

Do I need an architect’s drawing for a dropped kerb?

Not at the councils checked here that say what to send: Kent, Essex and Sheffield ask for a sketch or plan with measurements, and Hertfordshire for a good quality plan. A planning application, if you need one, has its own drawing requirements.
