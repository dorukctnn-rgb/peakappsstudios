How to export your Samsung Health data and open it on a PC

In the Samsung Health app, open Settings and tap Download personal data. The app saves a folder of CSV files on the phone, in My Files, Downloads, Samsung Health; nothing is emailed. Below: the steps, how to get the folder to a computer, and what each file in it means.

Export the data on the phone

These are the steps on Samsung’s support page (menu names can differ a little between app versions):

  1. Open Samsung Health, tap the three dots (More options), then Settings.
  2. Find Download personal data and tap Download. Allow the permissions it asks for, sign in to your Samsung account if asked, and wait until it finishes. Tap OK.
  3. Open My Files, then Downloads, then Samsung Health. The download is the folder inside named like samsunghealth_yourname_20261018091530: your account, then the date and time of the download.

Move it to a computer

  1. In My Files, touch and hold the folder, tap More, then Compress. A ZIP file appears next to the folder.
  2. Send the ZIP to yourself: by email, Google Drive, OneDrive, or Quick Share to a Windows PC. Or connect the phone with a USB cable, open it in File Explorer and copy the folder from Download\Samsung Health.
  3. On the computer you can keep it zipped. Wristory opens the ZIP, the folder or single CSV files in the browser and shows your steps, sleep, heart rate, weight and workouts; the files are never uploaded.

A download you request on Samsung’s privacy website (privacy.samsung.com) is different: it comes as a ZIP with a password that Samsung sends by email. Unzip it with that password first; browsers cannot open password-protected ZIPs.

What is in the folder

One CSV file per kind of data, named after Samsung’s data type and the time of the download, for example com.samsung.health.weight.20261018091530.csv. There are dozens of them; these are the ones most people look for:

The jsons folder holds the minute-by-minute detail behind some rows (heart rate through each hour, workout samples, routes). Files the app does not use for your account can be missing, and their columns change between app versions.

How to read the files

  • The first line is not the header. It describes the file (data type, version, number of columns). The column names are on the second line, and data starts on the third. Many rows end with an extra empty column.
  • Times are in UTC. A time like 2026-10-17 22:40:00.000 is UTC; the local offset is in a separate time_offset column (UTC+0900). Add the two to get the time you lived. Daily tables use day_time instead, a date or, in older downloads, a number of milliseconds.
  • Durations are in milliseconds. 1,800,000 is 30 minutes. Sleep durations in the sleep file are in minutes.
  • Some column names are long. Several files put the data type in front of the column name, as in com.samsung.health.heart_rate.heart_rate.

To open one file in Excel, use Data, From Text/CSV, choose UTF-8, comma as the delimiter, and skip the first row. In German or Portuguese Excel, where the comma is the decimal mark, pick the delimiter by hand. Wristory Pro writes one Excel workbook instead: a row per day with steps, sleep stages, heart rate, weight, stress and blood oxygen in plain units, and sheets for workouts, sleep, weight and blood pressure.

An older history sitting in Fitbit, now Google Health? It only leaves as a Google Takeout archive of JSON files; Tallyband turns that archive into CSV or Excel for the dates you choose.
