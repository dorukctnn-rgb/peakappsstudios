Menopause adjustments at work, and a letter to ask for them

Choose what affects you at work. You see the changes that Acas and the GOV.UK guidance for employers list, each with its source, and a polite letter asking your manager for a conversation about the ones you pick.

  • Ideas from Acas and GOV.UK guidance
  • Nothing you choose is stored or sent
  • Free, with no sign-up

Is menopause covered by the Equality Act?

“Menopause is not a specific protected characteristic under the Equality Act 2010,” says Acas. The Equality and Human Rights Commission says:

If menopause or perimenopause symptoms have a long term and substantial impact on a woman’s ability to carry out normal day-to-day activities, these symptoms could be considered a disability. If menopause or perimenopause symptoms amount to a disability, an employer will be under a legal obligation to make reasonable adjustments.

EHRC, Menopause in the workplace: guidance for employers, updated 24 June 2026

Whether that applies to you depends on your circumstances, and this tool does not decide it. Acas and the EHRC add that less favourable treatment because of menopause symptoms could also be age or sex discrimination. For advice, the Acas helpline is 0300 123 1100, Monday to Friday, 8am to 6pm. This page is not legal advice.

    Where the ideas come from
    
      
    Acas. Its guidance on menopause at work lists changes an employer could make, from flexible start and finish times to control over your working environment, and the points a menopause risk assessment covers. It also says changes should be agreed in writing and reviewed, and that absence because of menopause should be recorded separately.
    
      
    GOV.UK. The action pages written for employers’ equality action plans: workplace adjustments, the menopause risk assessment, occupational health advice and support networks. They suggest employers respond to requests as soon as possible and agree changes in writing.
    
      
    The NHS. The list of symptoms, in its words. The match between a symptom and a change is ours, to help you choose.
    
    
    
    
    
      
    Before you send it
    
      
    Talking first is fine. Acas says employers should invite workers to talk privately, and that you decide how much to share. The letter can simply start that conversation.
    
      
    Longer-term changes. For a lasting change to your hours or pattern, Acas says you could make a flexible working request.
    
      
    Help and advice. A trade union representative, HR or an employee assistance service can help. For advice on your rights, call the Acas helpline on 0300 123 1100.

    Questions
    
    
    
      
    Is menopause covered by the Equality Act?
    Menopause is not itself a protected characteristic under the Equality Act 2010, Acas says. The Equality and Human Rights Commission says that if menopause or perimenopause symptoms have a long term and substantial impact on a woman’s ability to carry out normal day-to-day activities, they could be considered a disability, and if they amount to a disability the employer is under a legal obligation to make reasonable adjustments. Less favourable treatment because of menopause symptoms could also be age or sex discrimination. Whether any of this applies to you depends on your circumstances.
    
      
    What are reasonable adjustments for menopause at work?
    Acas lists changes such as flexible start and finish times, breaks when needed, a private area to rest, working from home when practical, time off on a day you cannot carry on, changes to some duties, and control over your working environment such as a fan or a window that opens. A menopause risk assessment looks at temperature and ventilation, uniforms, rest areas, toilets and cold drinking water.
    
      
    Do I have to tell my employer about my symptoms?
    Acas says the worker should decide how much information they share, and that managers should respect their privacy and not tell colleagues without their permission. The letter here mentions your symptoms only if you choose to.
    
      
    Who can I talk to if not my manager?
    Acas says employers should give the option of talking to someone other than your manager, such as someone in HR, a trade union representative, an employee assistance counsellor or a menopause or wellbeing champion. For advice on your rights at work, the Acas helpline is 0300 123 1100.
