What the file needs

  1. One row per worker, with their sex and their category of workers: the group doing the same work or work of equal value. A job evaluation sets the categories.
  2. Basic pay and complementary or variable pay for the year, and the hours paid or weekly hours. A gross hourly rate also works.
  3. If you have them: a full-time equivalent column, the job title and the name, for the letter.

The file is read by this page on your computer. It is not uploaded, and nothing about its contents is sent anywhere.

    How the figures are worked out

    Pay level. Gross annual pay is basic pay plus complementary or variable pay for the year; gross hourly pay is that divided by the hours paid (Article 3(1)(a) and (b)). Weekly hours are multiplied by 52.18, as in the Pay Gap Report.

    Averages. The mean for the women and for the men in the worker's category, over everyone the data checks keep: recorded as male or female, with pay and hours. The worker who asked is part of their own group.

    Part-time. Hourly pay is unaffected by hours worked. Annual pay can be shown as paid or converted to full time: divided by the FTE in your file, or by the hours paid over a full-time year.

    Groups of one or two

    The directive sets no minimum group size. It lets each country decide that, where an answer would disclose an identifiable worker's pay, only workers' representatives, the labour inspectorate or the equality body see it (Article 12(3)). Malta has used that option; Slovakia leaves out an average that would reveal another employee's pay; Germany's current law leaves out the comparison pay where fewer than six people of the other sex do the work.

    The tool flags what follows from the arithmetic: a group of one is that person's pay, and a group of two that includes the worker who asked lets them work out the other's. It leaves those figures out of the letter unless you choose to include them.

    Questions

    Is the payroll file uploaded anywhere?

    No. The file is read by JavaScript in this page, on your computer, and the figures are worked out there. Nothing about the file is sent to a server. If you buy Pro, only the licence key is sent to Gumroad to check it.

    Which averages does the answer give?

    By default the directive's own definition of pay level: gross annual pay and the corresponding gross hourly pay (Article 3(1)(b)), each as the mean for the women and for the men in the worker's category. You can give annual pay only or hourly pay only, and show annual pay as paid or converted to full time. The letter says which you chose.

    How is part-time work handled?

    Hourly pay divides annual pay by the hours paid, so part-time work does not lower it. Annual pay is shown as paid unless you choose full-time equivalent, which divides it by the FTE from your file, or by hours against the full-time week you set.

    What if a category has only one or two people of one sex?

    The tool flags it. An average over one person is that person's pay, and in a group of two that includes the worker who asked, the worker can work out the other person's pay. The directive lets countries route such figures only to workers' representatives, the labour inspectorate or the equality body (Article 12(3)); it sets no minimum group size. You decide whether to leave the figure out, and the letter says so.

    Does it handle Germany's Entgelttransparenzgesetz?

    Yes. For establishments with more than 200 employees it works out the comparison pay of § 11(3): the median of the average monthly gross pay of the other sex in the comparison activity, converted to full time, and leaves it out where fewer than six of them do that work (§ 12(3)). The answer is due within three months under § 15(3).
