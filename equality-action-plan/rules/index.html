Equality action plan rules: minimums, word limits and deadlines

The 18 actions, in GOV.UK’s five groups

Minimums and statuses

You must choose a minimum of 2 actions in your action plan: one must address your gender pay gap; one must support employees experiencing menopause.

GOV.UK, Step 2. Choose your actions
New or in progress
You will be working on it for the first time, or building on something you already do. At least 2 actions must have this status. The published plan lists them as planned actions.
Embedded
Already an established part of your working practice. You can add as many as apply; they show what you have already done, but do not count towards the 2 new or in progress actions.

So 1 embedded pay gap action and 1 embedded menopause action is not enough: the plan also needs 2 actions that are new or in progress. Once plans are mandatory, Step 6 adds that you must be working on at least 2 actions at any time, so changing an action’s status or removing one means adding a new action.

Supporting text and word limits

  • Each new or in progress action must have supporting text on why your organisation chose it and how you will track and understand whether it is improving gender equality.
  • Embedded actions can have text on how your organisation implemented the action and what the results have been.
  • Up to 100 words for each action, with no links and no files such as CSVs or PDFs.
  • An overall supporting narrative of up to 200 words, optional, for background and your objectives for gender equality. No files.
  • A link to your own website, asked for separately, for example to your equality policies or strategy. GOV.UK encourages you to publish the plan’s information on your own site too.

How words are counted. The gender pay gap service loads the GOV.UK Design System, whose word counter counts every run of characters between spaces or line breaks as one word. So “part-time” is one word, “4 April 2027” is three, and a dash or an ampersand with a space either side counts as one. The builder counts the same way; the service makes the final check.

The responsible person, and groups of companies

Private and voluntary sector employers must include the name of a responsible person when they submit the plan. GOV.UK says the responsible person should usually be a director, partner or senior officer, and “will be responsible for confirming that the information you have submitted is accurate”. Most public authority employers do not need to do this.

An organisation with several legal entities must submit a plan for each entity with 250 or more employees. Each can have its own actions and text, or the same actions and narratives can be copied into a plan for each, for example where one diversity and inclusion strategy covers the whole group.

Who, where and when

  • Employers with 250 or more employees, the same threshold as gender pay gap reporting. Section 78A does not apply below 250.
  • Public authorities only if they are listed in Part 1 of Schedule 19 to the Equality Act 2010, or in Part 4 with the letter “D” after the entry. Public authorities in Scotland and Wales have different rules.
  • England, Scotland and Wales. Section 78A extends to England and Wales and Scotland.
  • Deadlines for the 2026 to 2027 reporting year: 30 March 2027 for most public authority employers (snapshot date 31 March 2026), 4 April 2027 for private, voluntary and all other public authority employers (snapshot date 5 April 2026). When plans become mandatory.

Questions

Can we write our own actions?

The guidance asks you to choose from the government’s list of 18 actions and does not describe adding your own. You can say how you will carry out each one in its supporting text, and add background in the overall narrative.

Can an embedded action count towards the two required actions?

It counts as one of your actions, and an embedded menopause action meets the menopause minimum. But at least 2 actions must be new or in progress, so a plan of one embedded pay gap action and one embedded menopause action does not meet the rules.

Can we put a link in the supporting text?

No. Step 3 says you cannot add links or upload files in the text for each action. The service asks separately for a link to the relevant page on your own website, such as your equality policies or strategy.
