Design a sim racing cockpit around your body

Sim Rig Designer is a free online sim rig design tool. Enter your height, your seat, your wheel base and your screens, and it builds the rig in 3D, checks your knee, hip and elbow angles against published driving-posture studies, and lists every board, bracket and bolt to buy.

    Questions

    What are the right dimensions for a sim racing cockpit?

    They depend on your body and on how you sit. For GT and road driving, the 55 drivers in Porter and Gyi's adjustable rig (1998) chose a hip point 283 to 335 mm (11 to 13 in) above the heel and a seat back 5 to 25 degrees from upright; SAE J1100 gives cars 127 to 405 mm. Formula seats sit lower and recline further. Sim Rig Designer places the seat, pedals, wheel and screen for your own height and checks the angles. Dimensions by height and style

    What angle should my knees be at in a sim rig?

    Fanatec suggests about 100 to 120 degrees for GT, with a slight bend left even at full brake. Drivers in Porter and Gyi's rig chose 99 to 138 degrees; Rebiffé gives 95 to 135 and Grandjean 110 to 130. The tool works the angle out from your leg lengths, at rest and with the brake down.

    How high should the steering wheel be?

    Asetek: around chest to shoulder height, with your shoulders relaxed and your elbows slightly bent; Qubic puts a GT wheel's centre near the base of the neck. Fanatec gives 90 to 120 degrees at the elbow. In a formula position the wheel may sit higher relative to the torso. The tool also checks that the rim and the wheel deck clear your thighs and knees.

    Is a 2x4 sim rig strong enough for a direct drive wheel?

    No published figure says how stiff a wheel deck must be. Asetek warns that a direct drive base's stronger force feedback can expose flex in a setup. The tool braces the deck (posts, braces and an apron in wood; 40 x 80 posts with a cross bar in aluminium) and asks you to grip the rim and twist hard once it is built. The 2x4 rig, step by step

    Does the tool know my wheel base's bolt pattern?

    No. It stores no maker's bolt pattern. A diagram shows how to measure the holes under your base, pedals and shifter, and the plates and the DXF files use exactly what you type; until you tick measured, the checks say the pattern is an example. Cutting the plates

    Is anything I enter sent to a server?

    No. The design, the checks and the 3D model are worked out in your browser. The share link keeps the design after the # sign, which browsers do not send to servers. If you buy Pro, only the licence key is sent to Gumroad to check it. We count page visits with Cloudflare Web Analytics: no cookies, no personal data, and never your files.
