Aluminum sim rig plans in 40-series profile

A GT rig for a driver 6 ft tall, with a direct drive wheel, load cell pedals, a shifter, a handbrake and three 27 in screens, built in 40 x 80 and 40 x 40 profile: 32 pieces, 90 corner brackets and 210 T-nuts, each counted from the design.

Why 40 x 80 under the seat and the wheel

The two base rails and the two wheel-deck posts are 40 x 80; everything else is 40 x 40. The reason is in RS PRO's profile datasheet: stood on its narrow face, 40 x 80 has a second moment of area of 70.07 cm⁴, against 9.18 cm⁴ for 40 x 40, so it bends about 7.6 times less under the same load. Laid flat it drops to 16.93 cm⁴, which is why the tool stands every 40 x 80 piece on edge, in the direction the wheel and your weight push.

The profile, cut by cut

Profile is ordered cut to length in millimetres, so the lengths are given that way, with inches alongside. Every cut is square; the braces meet the frame through angle brackets, so nothing needs a mitre.

Slide the T-nuts into the slots before you close a frame: a drop-in T-nut can go in later, but a slide-in one cannot once both ends of the slot are blocked. The seat's brackets or sliders bolt to the two seat bars with M8 bolts into T-nuts as well.

The plates and the gear

The wheel, pedal and shifter plates here are 6 mm aluminium, cut from the DXF files the tool writes, 4 holes for the wheel base, 6 for the pedals and 8 for the shifter and handbrake. Their fixings are slots over the profile, so each plate can slide a little before its M8 bolts are tightened into T-nuts. How the plates are drawn and cut covers the files, the hole patterns and the cutting; the bolts into your gear depend on its threads, so the shopping list leaves their size to you.

Questions

How much aluminum profile does a sim rig need?

This GT rig with a handbrake and three screens on a stand takes 4.61 m of 40 x 80 and 12.76 m of 40 x 40, about 36.4 kg. The screen stand alone is a good part of the 40 x 40; without it, or with a single screen, the total drops.

Why two corner brackets on every joint?

One bracket holds a joint at one corner, so the joint can still twist about the bolt. A bracket on each side of the face stops that. Where an 80 mm face meets the frame, the tool counts four, two in each slot.

Do I need 40 x 80 at all?

You can build it all in 40 x 40, but the base rails and the wheel posts carry the most bending, and 40 x 80 on edge resists it about 7.6 times better (RS PRO datasheet figures). The tool uses 40 x 80 exactly there.

Which T-nuts and bolts fit?

This design uses profile with an 8 mm slot throughout, M8 T-nuts made for that slot and M8 button-head bolts. Check the slot width of the profile you buy before ordering the hardware: brackets and T-nuts are made for one slot size.
