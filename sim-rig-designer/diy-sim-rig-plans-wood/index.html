DIY sim rig plans in 2x4 wood

A GT cockpit for a driver 5 ft 10 in tall, built from 8 boards of 2x4 and 1 sheet of plywood, with the seat, pedals, wheel and screen placed for that body. Open it in Sim Rig Designer, type your own height, and every length below changes to suit.

What you are building

Two 2x4 rails stood on edge make the base, 4 ft 5 1/8 in long, with cross members screwed between them. Everything else is built on that base, part by part, in the order you would build it:

  • Seat mount. Two bars laid across the rails on risers 8 3/4 in tall. A bucket seat's side brackets or a car seat's sliders bolt through the bars with M8 bolts, which puts the hip point 19 1/4 in above the floor.
  • Pedal deck. Two stringers laid flat and tilted 3 degrees, a cross brace under the front edge, and a plywood plate 25 1/16 in wide on top. Your heels rest 26 1/4 in ahead of the hip point.
  • Wheel deck. Two posts carry a pair of rails tilted to 18 degrees, braced down to the base ahead of them, with a strip of plywood between the posts above your knees. The wheel base bolts to a plate 25 1/16 in by 11 7/16 in.
  • Shifter arm. An arm out from the right-hand rail, a post at its end and a brace back to the rail, under a small plate for the shifter.
  • Screen stand. Two feet, two posts and a bar, free of the rig, so the screen does not shake when the wheel kicks. The bar is 47 9/16 in up to the screen's centre.

Screws, bolts and glue

  • Wood screws, 3 in: 232 screws. Four at each joint, plus a tenth spare. Drill pilot holes near the ends.
  • Wood screws for the plates and panels, 1 1/2 in: 33 screws. Every fixing hole in the plates, about twelve for each panel, plus a tenth spare.
  • Wood glue: 1 bottle. On every panel joint and cleat.
  • M8 bolts, washers and nyloc nuts through the seat bars: 4 sets. Check the thread your seat brackets or sliders take.
  • A VESA bracket for the screen on the bar: 1 bracket. Match the VESA pattern on the back of your screen.
  • Bolts, washers and nuts for the wheel deck plate (6.5 mm holes): 4 sets. Bolt size to suit the threads in your gear.
  • Bolts, washers and nuts for the pedal plate (8.5 mm holes): 6 sets. Bolt size to suit the threads in your gear.

Is 2x4 stiff enough for the wheel?

This design has a belt-drive wheel base, which pulls on the deck less than a direct drive motor does. No study or standard gives a number for how stiff a wheel deck has to be, so the tool does not pretend to calculate it. What it does: braces the posts down to the base, ties them together with the plywood strip, and, when you choose a direct drive base, adds a check that asks you to grab the rim and twist it hard once the rig stands. If the deck moves, add a brace before you race. Asetek's ergonomics guide makes the same point from the other side: the stronger the force feedback, the more any flex in a setup shows.

Build order

  1. Base frame. Cut the two side rails and the cross members, stand the rails on edge and screw the cross members between them, two screws a side into pilot holes. Square the frame by its diagonals.
  2. Seat mount. Fix the two seat bars across the rails on their risers (8 3/4 in tall), then bolt the seat brackets or sliders to them. Sit in it before you go on: the hip point should be 19 1/4 in above the floor.
  3. Pedal deck. Fix the stringers at 3 degrees on their spacer blocks, the cross brace under the front, then the pedal plate. Your heels rest 2 3/8 in from its back edge.
  4. Wheel deck. Stand the two posts on the rails, fix the deck rails across their tops at the deck angle, the braces down to the rails ahead, and glue and screw the front panel between the posts. Screw the wheel plate on top.
  5. Shifter. Fix the arm out from the right side rail, the post at its end, the brace back to the rail, and the plate on top.
  6. Screen stand. Build the stand: two feet, two posts and the bar across at 47 9/16 in to its centre. Stand it so the screen is 33 3/4 in from your eyes.
  7. Gear and fit. Bolt the wheel base, the pedals and the shifter to their plates, sit in, and check each angle in the checks table. Adjust the seat, the deck angles and the screen until they fit.

The plan assumes a bucket seat with side brackets. A car seat on sliders sits higher and an office chair needs the plywood wheel stand instead; pick your seat type in the tool before you buy wood, and every height follows it.

Questions

How many 2x4s does a sim rig take?

This GT rig takes 8 boards of 2x4 at 8 ft: 54.4 ft of pieces, with 15% left as offcut. A taller driver, a handbrake or a second screen changes the count, so the tool packs the boards again for every design.

Can I use 2x3 instead of 2x4?

Yes, the tool offers 2x3, which is 1 1/2 by 2 1/2 in once planed. The rails and posts are then an inch shallower and bend more under load, so it suits a belt or gear wheel better than a strong direct drive base. Do the twist test either way.

What tools do I need?

A saw that cuts square ends (a miter saw, or a circular saw with a speed square), a drill with a pilot bit for the screws, a jigsaw for the plates' rounded corners and any slots, a tape measure, a square and two clamps.

Is the seat part of the plan?

No. The plan carries a bucket seat with side brackets or a car seat on sliders on its seat bars; for an office chair, the tool has a plywood wheel stand instead. Pick the seat type in the tool and it places the bars, and the hip point, to suit.
