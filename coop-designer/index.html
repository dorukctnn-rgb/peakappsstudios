Design a chicken coop for your flock and your yard

Coop Designer is a free online chicken coop design tool. Enter your flock and your yard and it builds the coop and run in 3D from standard lumber, checks the space per hen, the nest boxes, the roosts and the hardware cloth, and lists every board and roll to buy.

    Questions

    How big should a chicken coop be?

    The extension guides we read give 1.5 to 4 sq ft of coop floor for each standard hen and 0.75 to 3 sq ft for each bantam, plus 4 to 10 sq ft of run a bird. The most recent table, Virginia Tech's of 2023, gives 3 to 4 sq ft inside and 10 sq ft outside for laying hens. Coop Designer sizes a new coop at 4 sq ft and the run at 10 sq ft a hen. Sizes for 3 to 30 hens

    How many nesting boxes do chickens need?

    One for every 3 to 5 hens: one per 4 to 5 (Maryland, Virginia Tech), one per 4 (Oregon State), one per 5 (Maine, Florida). Make each box about 12 in square, about 2 ft off the ground, with the roost higher so the hens sleep there and not in the boxes. Nest boxes by flock size

    Should I use hardware cloth or chicken wire?

    Hardware cloth. Utah State's extension wildlife specialist says chicken wire is not a barrier to predators and recommends 1/4 to 1/2 in hardware cloth; UConn Extension says to secure coops with hardware cloth, not chicken wire. Bury it at least 12 in deep, or lay it 1 ft down and 1 ft out in an L (Colorado State). Hardware cloth compared

    Is anything I enter sent to a server?

    No. The design, the checks and the 3D model are worked out in your browser. The share link keeps the design after the # sign, which browsers do not send to servers. If you buy Pro, only the licence key is sent to Gumroad to check it.

    Does it work in metric?

    Yes. Switch to metric and the frame uses 45 x 45 mm, 38 x 63 mm CLS, 38 x 89 mm CLS or 45 x 90 mm timber in lengths from 2.4 m to 4.8 m, with 13 mm welded mesh in 900 mm rolls and 2440 x 1220 mm plywood.

    Will the coop hold up to snow and wind?

    The tool does not size the frame for snow or wind. If you get heavy snow or strong wind, ask a local builder, anchor the coop and keep snow off the roof.
